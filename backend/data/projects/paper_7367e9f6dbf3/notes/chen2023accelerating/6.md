# chen2023accelerating.6.txt Analysis (Pages 7–8)

## Problem & Method
- **Problem**: Decoding efficiency of large language models; trade-off between draft length and scoring overhead.
- **Method**: Speculative sampling with modified rejection scheme; draft model samples K tokens, large model scores them; acceptance ratio measured as tokens accepted divided by (K + 1).
- **Key finding**: Average tokens accepted / (K + 1) measures overall efficiency; decreases as K increases due to dependency on prior token acceptance.

## Reported Results
- **Performance**: Average time per loop increases approximately linearly with K due to more model calls.
- **Speedup trade-off**: Increasing K reduces scoring calls from large model but increases total loop time linearly; overall speedup plateaus or degrades with larger K.
- **Specific example**: XSum with nucleus latency minimized at K = 3.
- **Variance**: Larger K increases variance of time to generate full sequence; problematic for P90/P99 latency-sensitive settings.
- **Empirical validation**: Technique lossless downstream; verified with Chinchilla 70B using easy-to-train draft model.

## Limitations
- Efficiency decreases with larger K due to token dependency on prior acceptance.
- Speedup plateaus or degrades beyond optimal K (e.g., K = 3 for XSum).
- Increased variance in generation time harms P90/P99 latency scenarios.
