Considerations for human performance measurements. Benchmark design choices, such as the
evaluation framework, can affect human performance measurements. If a benchmark has high variability in possible responses, human performance is likely to be lower, but this does not necessarily
mean the benchmark is extraordinarily difficult. An example of this is SQuAD 1.1, which takes a
span of a passage as an answer, and has a human accuracy of 77.0% (Rajpurkar et al., 2016). While
this may suggest that the task is difficult, the state-of-the-art performance has actually far exceeded
human performance, suggesting the opposite. When analyzing human responses, the authors found
that errors typically occurred when humans omitted unnecessary words or phrases from their answer spans. Thus, the low human performance may have largely resulted from limitations of the
evaluation framework rather than the difficulty of the task.
The method of collecting human performance should also be considered. Human performance
may be lower than expected if the measurement is crowdsourced, which is typical for recent benchmarks. Crowd workers want to complete tasks quickly to maximize their earnings, and thus may not
spend as much time reasoning through the examples as they would in an everyday setting. Because
of this, as discussed in Section 2.2.2, an additional human performance measurement collected from
authors of the dataset or other experts is sometimes provided as a ceiling. However, the difference
