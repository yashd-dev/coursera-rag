C.1. Removing the correction from a trained model
For each of the four higher-rate residual models, we compare corrected and pre-correction logits
from the same forward pass, keeping the trained backbone and query/key projections fixed.
The head computes both sets of logits in FP32 without autocast, so the comparison isolates the
correction’s contribution at inference within an already trained model.
Removing the correction changes only four of 185,900 selected answers, with no correct
answer becoming incorrect, and increases mean MC11 by less than 0.001 pp. This mean uses the
same configuration weighting as the main evaluation. The learned bilinear component therefore
recovers virtually all answer selections at these checkpoints.
C.2. Frozen branches, repeated training, and divergence
The training comparisons show a stronger dependence on the recipe. At the higher rate, the
frozen-branch model exceeds the pointer by 1.39 ± 0.38 pp on MC11, and the trained residual
model exceeds the frozen-branch model by a further 2.02 ± 0.97 pp. Under the lower-rate recipe,
the core comparisons are small and depend on the reference run (Table 11), while the separate
comparison with jointly reduced head and adapter rates retains a +0.57 ± 0.18 pp residual
advantage.
