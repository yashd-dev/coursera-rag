The head-by-adapter rate interaction is +1.28 ± 1.65 pp on MC11. The pooling and no-FFN
main comparisons retain positive four-setting means under both weighting rules and every
source omission. The interaction between them is less stable: omitting chess reverses its MC11weighted mean. Likewise, no-FFN rank 64 versus all-module rank 28 can change sign under
configuration-weighted omission. The near-zero head-rate and trained-residual means change
sign under equal source weighting. These outcomes support the individual recipe comparisons
more strongly than claims about their interactions or the ranking of near-equal arms.
The budget result also persists under reweighting: longer training lowers both models’
accuracy, with the pointer losing more. ARC and HellaSwag account for about 63% of the
wider gap. Under seven-source weighting, the mean interaction remains +3.66 pp, although one
setting has a small negative interaction of −0.20 pp.
Replacing the reference pointer by its repeated training changes the all-module pooling
effect to +0.55 ± 1.11 pp and the trained-residual effect to −0.12 ± 0.58 pp. No-FFN and rank-28
pointers retain gains of +2.04 ± 0.63 and +1.95 ± 0.77 pp, each positive in all four settings. These
larger recipe gains are less dependent on which reference execution is used.
