6.2. Readout on Qwen3-4B
The Qwen3-4B comparison tests the readout change under lower-rate, all-module adaptation.
Pooling improves HellaSwag by 3.91 pp, close to the 4.23 pp gain on NeoHorse, and also
improves the ARC configurations. Declines elsewhere, including a 4.28 pp loss on four-option
GSM8K, outweigh these gains: overall MC11 accuracy is 51.70% for pooling and 52.17% for the
pointer. The readout change thus retains gains on particular tasks without improving aggregate
accuracy on this backbone. Appendix B reports the complete task comparison.

7. Trade-offs on Direct Selection
We also test the same core checkpoints under direct selection to see how the reranking choices
affect their original use (Table 6). The no-FFN pointer raises MC11 while maintaining JevBench
accuracy, at 77.52% versus 77.11% for the all-module pointer. Adding pooling improves MC11
further, with JevBench and Kev accuracies of 73.79% and 78.12%. These are 3.32 and 3.25 pp
below the all-module pointer.
Across both adapter scopes, the pointer gives higher direct-selection accuracy than pooling
on these suites. Kev’s declines include decision-test as well as the transfer subsets; Appendix B
gives the six-subset profile. Together with the fixed-checkpoint interface comparisons in Section 4, these results support choosing the readout with the intended scoring task in mind.

8. Related Work
Studies of specialized JEV decision models report uneven performance across knowledge,
reasoning, and multilingual tasks [24]. Interventions on labels, definitions, and context also
change their behavior without changing model weights [1, 47]. LLM-as-Jev examines joint suffix
scoring without training and fine-tuning with KL anchors [25]. Related work on prompted
language models shows that multiple-choice scores depend on the scoring interface and option
identifiers [34, 53]. We focus on improving the trained JEV model when it is used to compare
candidate answers through separate entailment requests.
Pointer and biaffine architectures score contextual representations with shared projections
[44, 14]. Research on sentence and span representations examines which token states to aggregate
