Our experiments on NeoHorse-1-4B show that the scoring interface changes the relative
performance of option readouts, while reducing adaptation capacity can improve reranking
accuracy. With checkpoints and questions held fixed on PIQA and SocialIQA, the pointer
leads on average under direct selection, while pooling leads under entailment reranking. On
the eleven-configuration MC11 benchmark, the one-epoch, lower-rate recipe establishes an
all-module pointer reference at 50.28% accuracy. Selective adaptation raises this to 52.49% while
reducing trainable parameters by 55.2% (from 131.2M to 58.8M), and adding mean-span pooling
lifts accuracy further to 53.39% at the same parameter count. Both steps improve MC11 across
all four fold–seed settings. Under all-module adaptation, mean-span pooling (51.00%) also
outperforms a pointer at the final content token (49.11%). The individual design choices also
improve PIQA and CommonsenseQA reranking, whereas the combined model loses accuracy
on the JevBench and Kev direct-decision suites.
