Additional-source protocol. PIQA and CommonsenseQA evaluate 1,838 validation and 1,221
development questions through NLI reranking. CLINC150 [21] evaluates all 4,500 in-scope test
utterances through a single 150-option direct request. These evaluations cover 36 models varying
learning rates, training schedules, or adapter placement, plus eight separately trained pointers
comparing adapter scopes with matched initialization. The final no-FFN pooling configuration
is evaluated on MC11, JevBench, and Kev.
CommonsenseQA uses the question stem without its question-concept field; each of its
five candidate answers becomes “The correct answer is: [candidate]”. The 24 questions with
duplicate answer texts retain distinct candidate identities. Reranking candidate order follows
the SHA-256 of canonical JSON triples containing the source revision, sample identity, and
candidate identity, with candidate identity as a secondary key. Gold labels enter neither requests
nor ordering. Exact ties use the first maximum. Uniform-tie credit leaves the reported pooling
and scope effects unchanged. Registered training and development-selection materials contain
no exact or normalized PIQA or CommonsenseQA question matches under Unicode NFKC, case,
and whitespace normalization; semantic overlap and pretraining exposure remain unmeasured.
On CLINC150, pooling lowers accuracy by 3.64 ± 0.54 pp in the lower-rate readout comparison, and omitting FFN adapters lowers it by 1.78 ± 0.41 pp in the separate scope comparison.

known CLINC overlap item is retained and correctly classified by every model, contributing
zero to each paired difference.
