4.2 Early Statistical Approaches
Statistical approaches dominated the NLP field from the mid 1990s until the early 2010s. These earlier statistical approaches relied on engineered features to train various types of statistical models
based on training data for a variety of early NLP benchmarks, e.g., question answering benchmarks like the classic Remedia reading comprehension dataset (Hirschman et al., 1999) and the
TREC datasets (Voorhees, 2000), which were largely based on linguistic context and thus outside
of the scope of this survey. Notable early approaches often combined various word matching and
other lexical features with handcrafted deterministic rules Charniak, Altun, Braz, Garrett, Kosmala,
Moscovich, Pang, Pyo, Sun, Wy, Yang, Zeller, and Zorn (2000), or with traditional statistical models like decision trees (Ng, Teo, & Kwan, 2000). Similar approaches were also applied to some of
the benchmarks we survey here, beginning with the RTE Challenges (Dagan et al., 2005). Lexical
features, for example, based on bag-of-words and word matching were commonly used in the first
two RTE Challenges (Dagan et al., 2005; Bar-Haim et al., 2006), but often achieved results only
slightly better than random guessing (Bar-Haim et al., 2006). More competitive systems have used
more linguistic features to make predictions, such as semantic dependencies and paraphrases (Hickl,
