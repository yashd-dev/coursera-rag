A.6. Evidence coverage and reproduction
Model development used the primary evaluation suites and fold-development data. Because the
four reported settings share six training pools and the same evaluation questions, their standard
errors describe variation within this design. The original GPQA few-shot configuration draws
demonstrations from the Diamond evaluation population; Appendix B.4 reports a separate
evaluation with disjoint demonstrations.
Overlap screening found no exact or normalized state matches between the training corpus
and 231 JevBench records or 6,436 Kev record occurrences, using Unicode NFKC normalization,
case folding, and whitespace normalization. Available source identities likewise showed no
overlap. Further screening covered 30,503 reranking source rows, including 285 MMLU demonstration rows, and the 198-row GPQA file. This coverage is limited to the recorded source text
and identities; semantic paraphrases, substrings, pretraining exposure, synthetic chess, and
rendered evaluation requests remain unassessed. The intermediate SetFit Amazon conversion
has not been recovered.
The saved predictions support independent rescoring of 72 checkpoints: the 56 core NeoHorse models, eight Qwen3 models, and eight higher-rate pointer and pooling checkpoints used
in the fixed-interface comparison. Candidate probabilities are stored with answer identities, candidate order, population information, and eligibility rules, allowing predictions and aggregate
scores to be recomputed while preserving repeated questions and duplicate answer texts. Some
additional learning-rate and adapter-scope results are available only as per-model summaries.
Reproduction requires the base weights, training records, evaluation caches, framework
forks, and compatible runtimes specified by the code and configurations. The retained inputs
supported reconstruction of the corpus mixture and fold exports, followed by one separate
pointer/pooling pair from training through scoring on H20. The OpenCompass and RunWeaver
forks are unavailable anonymously, and redistribution permissions for RunWeaver and other
assets remain unresolved. The initial source-generation and tokenizer-based eligibility steps
remain unreplayed, and the tested environment retains a declared Torch dependency conflict.
CUDA determinism was disabled, allowing repeated training runs to diverge despite
matched initial parameters and data streams. Dropout trajectories were not saved for the
experiments that varied the head and adapter rates together. Appendix C examines execution
variation through repeated-training controls. The 56 core NeoHorse models were trained on
individual GPUs of a shared eight-H20 server, using 94.46 GPU hours over the measured training intervals with allocated-memory peaks of 18.32–19.81 GiB. These measurements include
concurrent-job contention and diagnostic capture; evaluation and the additional experiments
fall outside the timing intervals.
