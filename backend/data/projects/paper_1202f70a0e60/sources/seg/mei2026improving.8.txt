9. Conclusion
Fixed-checkpoint comparisons show that direct selection and entailment reranking can favor
different option readouts, indicating that design choices for single-request selection need not
transfer unchanged to cross-request reranking. Motivated by this observation, we pair meanspan pooling over each NLI option with selective backbone adaptation restricted to attention and
DeltaNet projections, jointly adjusting how option states are extracted and how the backbone
is updated. Matched comparisons under a tuned one-epoch recipe establish the gains from
both choices, while pointer-position, parameter-budget, and head-capacity controls clarify their
respective contributions.
On NeoHorse-1-4B, selective adaptation improves MC11 from 50.28% to 52.49%, and adding
mean-span pooling reaches 53.39%, with both steps positive in all four fold–seed settings.
Together, these changes improve reranking accuracy by about 3.1 pp while reducing trainable
parameters by 55.2%.
